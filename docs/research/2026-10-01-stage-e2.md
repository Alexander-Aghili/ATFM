# Stage E2: forecast-driven warming on a long-gap agent fleet: 1 October 2026

Follows the inconclusive [stage E](2026-09-30-stage-e.md). Software at `e3b02bd`;
evidence in [results/gpu-stage-e2-2026-10-01](results/gpu-stage-e2-2026-10-01/).

**Outcome:** on one H100 SXM Pod, in a bracketed order (direct, ATFM, ATFM, direct),
both ATFM runs had a median TTFT of 0.96-1.13 s against 3.67-3.92 s for both direct runs
(about -72%), a mean of 2.63 s against 4.12-4.43 s (about -38%), and replay durations of
1,578-1,581 s against 1,612-1,671 s (about -4%). Because the direct runs bracket the ATFM
runs and agree with each other, run-order drift does not explain the difference. The
experiment does **not** separate warming from the proxy hop (no proxy-only arm), and it
has two runs per arm on one Pod and one workload. Compute cost about 8.2 USD.

## What changed from stage E

Stage E replayed a single agent whose calls left a median 1.6 s idle, far less than the
~8 s needed to warm its contexts; only 1 of 119 requests followed a gap of 10 s or more.
E2 replays a **fleet**: four complete Weka roots chosen for long idle gaps (at least 8 of
19-26 gaps lasting 10 s or more, contexts up to 114k tokens, 100 requests in total),
run concurrently so their contexts share a 24 GiB CPU tier
(`trace_selection.FLEET`; AIPerf `--num-sessions 4 --concurrency 4`, sequential
sampling, one JSON file per root). 43 of 100 requests follow a gap of 10 s or more. The
board's training table holds out all seven replayed roots (98,563 rows). ATFM used the
median-return trigger with re-warming after 30 s. The Pod (AP-IN-1, 28 vCPU) had a host
load average of about 3-8 throughout.

## Interpretation and limits

- **Drift is ruled out as the main cause.** The two direct runs (first and last) agree
  (median 3.67 and 3.92 s), and so do the two ATFM runs (1.13 and 0.96 s).
- **The gain is not confined to requests after long gaps.** Requests after gaps under
  2 s, where a session's own context had no time to be warmed, improved as much. A
  plausible mechanism is queueing: with two engine slots, a request waiting for an
  on-demand L2 load delays the others, and warming removes those loads from the critical
  path (stage C located load cost in vLLM queue time). This is not yet demonstrated.
- **The proxy is a confound.** ATFM runs route through the ATFM proxy; direct runs do
  not. The proxy could change admission or streaming behaviour. A proxy-only arm in the
  same bracket is required before attributing the gain to warming.
- **Tails and duration move less.** p95 TTFT was similar to the second direct run
  (8.1-8.4 s against 8.2 s), and duration improved by about 4%, because decode and
  recorded delays dominate total time.
- **Hit ratio fell under ATFM** (83.5-85.6% against 88.3-91.9%) while TTFT improved. The
  cause is not established; warms may displace other contexts, or the token-weighted
  ratio may move with request timing.
- Two runs per arm, one Pod, one four-root workload, one model, 24 GiB CPU tier.

## Next

Repeat on at least two Pods on separate hosts with a three-arm bracket
(direct, proxy, atfm, atfm, proxy, direct), and record vLLM queue time per request to
test the queueing mechanism. If the effect holds against the proxy-only arm, extend to
more fleet compositions and to the 48 GiB, 64-token-chunk baseline.
